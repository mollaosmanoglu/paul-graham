### Investors

<Note kind="essay" source="A Fundraising Survival Guide" href="http://www.paulgraham.com/fundraising.html" date="2008-08">
Investors evaluate startups the way customers evaluate products, not the way bosses evaluate employees.
</Note>

<Note kind="essay" source="A Fundraising Survival Guide" href="http://www.paulgraham.com/fundraising.html" date="2008-08">
Don't take rejection personally. Getting rejected by investors can make you start to doubt yourself.
</Note>

<Note kind="essay" source="A Fundraising Survival Guide" href="http://www.paulgraham.com/fundraising.html" date="2008-08">
Avoid inexperienced investors. Though novice investors seem unthreatening they can be the most dangerous sort, because they're so nervous.
</Note>

<Note kind="essay" source="High Resolution Fundraising" href="http://www.paulgraham.com/hiresfund.html" date="2010-09">
By far the biggest influence on investors' opinions of a startup is the opinion of other investors.
</Note>

<Note kind="essay" source="How to Raise Money" href="http://www.paulgraham.com/fr.html" date="2013-09">
You should always talk to investors in parallel rather than serially.
</Note>

<Note kind="essay" source="A Fundraising Survival Guide" href="http://www.paulgraham.com/fundraising.html" date="2008-08">
Don't just think "investors are stupid." Often they are, but figure out precisely where you lose them.
</Note>

<Note kind="essay" source="How to Raise Money" href="http://www.paulgraham.com/fr.html" date="2013-09">
You can increase the price for later investors, if you get a lot of interest, but by default the valuation you got from the first investor becomes your asking price.
</Note>

<Note kind="essay" source="Startup Investing Trends" href="http://www.paulgraham.com/invtrend.html" date="2013-06">
The reason I describe this as a danger is that series A investors are increasingly at odds with the startups they supposedly serve, and that tends to come back to bite you eventually.
</Note>

<Note kind="essay" source="How to Raise Money" href="http://www.paulgraham.com/fr.html" date="2013-09">
You have to estimate not just the probability that an investor will say yes, but the probability that they'd be the first to say yes, and the latter is not simply a constant fraction of the former.
</Note>

<Note kind="essay" source="How to Raise Money" href="http://www.paulgraham.com/fr.html" date="2013-09">

</Note>

<Note kind="essay" source="How to Fund a Startup" href="http://www.paulgraham.com/startupfunding.html" date="2005-11">

<Note kind="essay" source="How to Raise Money" href="http://www.paulgraham.com/fr.html" date="2013-09">

</Note>

<Note kind="essay" source="How to Raise Money" href="http://www.paulgraham.com/fr.html" date="2013-09">
